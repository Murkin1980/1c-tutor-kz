# International Accountant Track — Curriculum Skeleton

Status: planning hypothesis only
Date: 2026-08-15

## North Star

The learner must become capable of completing realistic international accounting work products correctly, not merely recalling IFRS rules.

## Progression

`1C Practice → Accounting Logic → Economic Substance → IFRS Treatment → Financial Statements → International Practice → Capability Assessment`

## Level 0 — Accounting English Basics

Capabilities:
- read common accounting labels and source documents;
- distinguish invoice / bill / statement / receipt / purchase order / credit note;
- understand debit, credit, ledger, journal, trial balance, receivable, payable, accrual, prepayment;
- translate familiar 1C/local concepts into practical English accounting terminology.

Primary assessment style:
- document interpretation;
- terminology mapping;
- identify transaction intent from English source documents.

## Level 1 — International Accounting Foundations

Capabilities:
- understand double-entry independent of a specific software package;
- map business events to accounts/ledger consequences;
- understand General Ledger, subledgers, AP, AR;
- prepare simple journal entries;
- trace a transaction from source document to ledger.

## Level 2 — Financial Statements

Capabilities:
- understand statement of financial position;
- profit or loss;
- cash-flow statement;
- changes in equity at foundation level;
- trial balance to statements bridge;
- identify where a transaction affects statements.

## Level 3 — Core IFRS

Build from familiar business topics rather than standard-number memorization.

Initial topic bridges:
- Inventory ↔ IAS 2;
- Property, plant and equipment ↔ IAS 16;
- Revenue ↔ IFRS 15;
- Leases ↔ IFRS 16;
- Cash flows ↔ IAS 7;
- Impairment ↔ IAS 36;
- Financial instruments ↔ IFRS 9.

Each topic must include:
- business situation;
- economic substance;
- recognition/measurement logic;
- accounting entry where appropriate;
- financial-statement impact;
- evidence-backed IFRS reference;
- practical exercise;
- capability assessment.

## Level 4 — Operational International Accounting

Capabilities:
- AP processing and reconciliation;
- AR processing and reconciliation;
- bank reconciliation;
- accruals;
- prepayments;
- fixed asset register;
- inventory accounting/reconciliation;
- basic revenue cut-off;
- supporting schedules;
- journal preparation/review.

## Level 5 — Month-End Close Simulator

Target work products:
- close checklist;
- bank reconciliation;
- AP/AR reconciliation;
- accrual schedule;
- prepaid expense schedule;
- fixed asset roll-forward;
- inventory reconciliation;
- review of unusual balances;
- trial balance after adjustments;
- close sign-off evidence.

Primary verification must read deterministic workspace state and work products, not quiz answers.

## Level 6 — IFRS Case Simulator

Cases combine multiple topics and incomplete/ambiguous evidence.

Learner must:
- identify relevant accounting questions;
- locate the applicable sourced IFRS topic;
- determine treatment;
- prepare calculation/schedule;
- prepare entry;
- explain financial-statement impact;
- document assumptions and unresolved issues.

## Level 7 — International Accountant Practice

Simulated operating environment:
- English source-document pack;
- accounting inbox/task queue;
- working papers;
- ledger/subledger extracts;
- month-end deadlines;
- review comments;
- corrections and re-performance.

Goal: work like an accountant, not like an exam candidate.

## Level 8 — Job / Freelance Readiness

Candidate verified capabilities:
- bank reconciliation independently;
- AP reconciliation independently;
- AR reconciliation independently;
- accrual preparation;
- prepaid schedule;
- fixed asset register/roll-forward;
- trial balance review;
- discrepancy investigation;
- supporting schedules;
- basic financial statements;
- IFRS treatment explanation with authoritative source;
- English-document processing;
- concise reviewer/client communication.

Final readiness should be evidenced by integrated work-sample cases.

## Prerequisite principle

Do not make the learner study IFRS as an isolated theory layer before practical foundations.

Where possible, unlock a new international topic from an already-proven local/1C capability.

Example:

`1C fixed-asset acquisition PASS`
→ `local accounting logic`
→ `asset recognition/measurement concept`
→ `IAS 16`
→ `fixed asset schedule`
→ `statement impact`
→ `international work-sample case`.

## Benchmark use

ACCA FA/FR/DipIFR materials may be used to benchmark expected depth and identify missing knowledge domains. They do not define the final Tutor sequence.

The final sequence must later be validated against real jobs, outsourcing workflows and practical assessments.
